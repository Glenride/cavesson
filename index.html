The security story

Name and story locked 26 October 2026.

A cavesson is the noseband a trainer puts on a powerful animal to keep it under a human's hand while it's being trained. It is the piece that says: this strength answers to a person.

Your data is your life. Your family. Your money. Your work. Your future. You wouldn't hand your house keys to a stranger, or let someone you don't know walk through your home and read your papers. So why is AI any different?

That's what happens when your agents live in someone else's cloud. Your data sits on machines you don't own, behind doors you don't lock. Hope is not a lock. Cavesson is the lock: your agents live in your home, behind your lock, under your hand. Every job is checked against what you asked for before anything goes out. Every job comes back with proof. Nothing leaves without your say.

Machines propose, but humans are the source of all decisions.

The next chapter is proof that doesn't require handing your data to anyone: the record of what an agent did, checked without seeing your data at all. Proof that protects you instead of exposing you.

What Cavesson is: a lock. A crew. A checkpoint. The thing that keeps a powerful team under a human's hand.

What Cavesson isn't: not a cloud. Not an open door. Not a black box. Not a replacement for the human.

The problem the Vault solves

An agent that only answers questions cannot lose money. An agent that can pay a vendor, hire help, and sign for work can. The moment an agent moves from chat to action, a vague instruction, a mistaken tool call, or a prompt that was never meant as permission can spend real money before any Human sees it.

  • No limit before the action. Agents are being given payment, hiring, and signing power without a Human-set limit written down first.
  • No receipt after the action. When something goes wrong, there is no clean receipt showing what was allowed, what was stopped, and who decided.
  • No middle ground. A founder who wants to use agents safely must either supervise every step or trust the agent outright. Neither scales.

How the Vault works

Three everyday parts, in order. The layers behind them are described on the architecture page.

  1. 1

    House rules, set by the Human first

    The Human sets a budget, an allowlist of what the agent may pay for, and who or what it may hire or sign for — before the agent runs.

  2. 2

    A gate, checked before execution

    Every proposed spend, hire, or signature is checked against those rules before it executes. Anything outside the rules is stopped, not warned about afterward.

  3. 3

    Attested receipts, for allowed and stopped actions

    Every action leaves a receipt: what was proposed, which rule applied, and what the Human decided. Stopped actions get receipts too — that is the point.

The two-minute demo

Exists as the simulated prototype walkthrough below — not a live-money demo. You can run the interactive prototype yourself; it is described in the status table further down this page.

The walkthrough

  1. The Human sets a $50 budget and an allowlist: research tools only, no contractors, no subscriptions.
  2. The agent proposes a $40 research purchase. The Vault allows it and shows the receipt.
  3. The agent then proposes a $200 contractor hire. The Vault stops it, names the rule it broke, and shows the stopped receipt to the Human.
  4. The Human raises the budget, or holds it. Either way, the decision and the receipt are on record.

Who the Vault is for

First, teams building agents that spend, hire, or sign

The first teams to work with the Vault are the ones already letting agents touch real budgets — starting with agent teams testing the Vault, then agent-infrastructure teams and small operators running agents on real budgets.

Not yet: broad merchant acceptance

The Vault is behind on merchant acceptance, micropayments, and enterprise proof. Enterprises that need those first are not the first audience, and this page does not pretend otherwise.

Pricing is deliberately open. No price is set. Pricing is to be validated with the first teams, not asserted here.
