Cavesson Vault

A wallet for founders, small operators, and their AI agents whose rules are 7 layers of protection. The Vault is the middle ground between supervising every step and trusting the agent outright: rules written first, a gate checked before anything moves, and a receipt after.

The problem

An agent that only answers questions cannot lose money. An agent that can pay a vendor, hire help, and sign for work can. A vague instruction, a mistaken tool call, or a prompt that was never meant as permission can spend real money before any Human sees it — because no limit was written down first, and no receipt exists after.

  • No limit before the action. Agents are being given payment, hiring, and signing power without a Human-set limit written down first.
  • No receipt after the action. When something goes wrong, there is no clean receipt showing what was allowed, what was stopped, and who decided.
  • No middle ground. A founder who wants to use agents safely must either supervise every step or trust the agent outright. Neither scales.

House rules, set by the Human first

Before the agent runs, the Human writes down:

  • A budget. How much the agent may spend, in total and per action.
  • An allowlist. What the agent may pay for — for example, research tools only, no contractors, no subscriptions.
  • Hire and sign permissions. Who or what the agent may hire or sign for, and what it may never hire or sign for.

Unapproved or unlabeled agents, rules, and vendors default to denied and stay excluded until the owner approves or labels them. Nothing pending is treated as approved.

A gate, checked before execution

Every proposed spend, hire, or signature is checked against the house rules before it executes. Inside the rules, the agent works freely. Outside them, it is stopped — not warned about afterward — and the stop goes to the Human for a decision: raise the limit, or hold it. Either way, the decision is on record.

The gate is also where identity is enforced. Every instance must carry Vault identity, and anything uncredentialed is treated as rogue at the gate by default. No identity means no passage. The layers that back the gate up are described on the architecture page.

Attested receipts, for allowed and stopped actions

Every action leaves a receipt naming three things: what was proposed, which rule applied, and what the Human decided. Stopped actions get receipts too — that is the point. A reviewer should be able to read what the Vault stopped without help.

Text alternative: agent proposal → rule check → either an allowed receipt (proposal, rule, Human decision) or a stopped receipt (proposal, rule broken, Human decision). The stopped path never moves money.

For whom

First, teams building agents that spend, hire, or sign — starting with the first agent teams testing the Vault, then agent-infrastructure teams and small operators running agents on real budgets.

Not yet: broad merchant acceptance. The Vault is behind on merchant acceptance, micropayments, and enterprise proof. Enterprises that need those first are not the first audience, and this page does not pretend otherwise.

Pricing is deliberately open. No price is set. Pricing is to be validated with the first teams, not asserted here. Revenue, when it comes, follows the wallet and rules service.

Prototype status, stated plainly

Prototype built 1 November 2026, probe-verified.

The full Vault prototype was built and probe-verified: a $40 research purchase was allowed, a $200 contractor hire was stopped, and a $75 purchase went to the queue and executed once the Human approved it.

Its limits, stated here rather than discovered later: the money is simulated, everything is in-memory, and it resets on reload. It is not live money and it is not shipped. The layered architecture behind the Vault — Layers 0–6 — remains design, not built, and the seven fundamentals are adopted, not implemented.

Try the interactive prototype

The 12-week build plan is a target, not a commitment: weeks 1–4, rules engine and wallet, proved by the two-minute demo running end to end; weeks 5–8, attested receipts and first agent teams; weeks 9–10, hardening; weeks 11–12, a live demo and first pricing conversations. If the demo cannot stop an overspend live by week 4, the plan changes in week 5.

What backs the gate up

The Vault's everyday work is rules, gate, and receipt. The seven layers behind it exist for the day an agent crosses the gate or goes rogue.
